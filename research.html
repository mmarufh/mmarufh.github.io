Research

Twisting two atom-thin layers changes their symmetry, and symmetry decides which optical signals a material can give off. So far my work has been about using that to switch nonlinear optical responses on and off, and about working out what a measurement should actually see when it happens.

Research Interests

Twistronics

A small twist between two layers creates a moiré pattern that reshapes their electronic bands. I like the idea of twist angle as a dial: it tunes electronic properties, and it can break symmetries that would otherwise stay locked in. Both my thesis and my first manuscript grew out of this.

Non-linear Optics

Second-harmonic generation is extremely sensitive to symmetry, which makes it a clean way to read out stacking, twist and polarization in 2D materials. In my work I predict how the second- and third-harmonic signals and the shift current should change with twist angle, light polarisation and gating. Those are the same knobs a polarisation-resolved SHG setup turns.

Low Energy Electronics

Designing an ALU with clock gating and voltage and frequency scaling gave me a real sense of how much energy goes into every switching event. Sliding ferroelectrics such as twisted hBN flip their polarization when one layer shifts slightly against the other, and I find them an exciting route to devices that switch with very little energy.

In Memory Computing

In many modern chips, moving data between memory and processor costs more energy than the computation itself. Non-volatile states, like the polarization domains in a ferroelectric 2D stack, could hold information right where it is processed. I'd like to bring together the materials I've studied and the circuit design I learned at BUET.

Research Projects

Under review · 2025 – 2026

Polar moiré potentials from twisted hBN switch on second-order optics

Twisted hBN carries a built-in pattern of up and down polarization. I modelled the electric potential this pattern creates, including how the graphene on top screens it, and fed that potential into the band structure of bilayer graphene. The result is a second-harmonic and shift-current response that simply isn't there without the substrate. We are now extending the same idea to MoSSe and MoS₂.

With Prof. M. Shah Alam, BUET
COMSOL · MATLAB continuum model · Wannier · WannierNLQG
B.Sc. thesis · 2025 – 2026

Even- and odd-order harmonics from twisted bilayer graphene

I modelled how twisting two graphene layers breaks inversion symmetry and lets even-order harmonics appear alongside the odd ones. Using first-principles input, Sipe's formulation and a continuum model, I looked at two-photon absorption and at how the second- and third-order responses change with twist angle.

Advisor: Prof. M. Shah Alam, BUET
First principles · Sipe formalism · Continuum model · MATLAB
Ongoing · 2025 – present

SHG and shift current in Janus MoSSe from first principles

Janus MoSSe has different atoms on its top and bottom faces, so it is polar by construction. I first reproduced its known second-harmonic and shift-current spectra, then started looking at what a twisted-hBN substrate adds, first for the monolayer and now for the bilayer.

DFT · Wannier tight-binding · WannierNLQG · Python
RISE, BUET · Undergraduate research

Light trapping in flexible kesterite solar cells

Thin, flexible solar cells lose a lot of light simply because the absorber is so thin. I studied how spherical nanoparticles can scatter light back into a kesterite absorber so that more of it gets absorbed.

Advisor: Dr. Ahmed Zubair, BUET
